Software bugs are often reported through technical support channels, but the quality and completeness of incoming reports can significantly affect how quickly developers understand and resolve problems. Reports may lack important information, such as steps to reproduce an issue, expected behavior, error messages, or relevant contextual details. As a result, developers may need to spend additional time clarifying the problem before they can begin investigating it.

The goal of this thesis is to develop and evaluate an intelligent technical support form designed to improve the process of identifying and resolving software bugs. The proposed solution will guide users through submitting structured bug reports and use a Large Language Model (LLM) to assist developers in interpreting the submitted information. The LLM will access the service's knowledge base and documentation through the Model Context Protocol (MCP), allowing it to provide recommendations grounded in the available technical information.

The effectiveness of the proposed solution will be evaluated by comparing it with a conventional technical support workflow. The evaluation will focus on the time required to understand and diagnose a reported problem, the completeness of the reports submitted, the accuracy of the suggested diagnosis, and the time needed to identify an appropriate solution. The expected result is an assessment of whether a structured support form combined with documentation-based AI assistance can improve the efficiency of software bug investigation and resolution.
\end{abstract}
